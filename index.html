A quick definition

Social engineering is the practice of manipulating a person, rather than a system, into doing something they normally wouldn't do, like handing over a password, approving a payment, or clicking a dangerous link. Phishing is the most common form of it: a fake email, text message, or website built to look routine and trustworthy enough that nobody stops to double check it before acting on it.

The Risk

Phishing turns your own employees into an attack surface.

Attackers don't need to break encryption or crack a firewall. They send an email that looks routine, an invoice, a password reset, a message from IT, and wait for one person to click. Industry breach research consistently points to the human element as a leading factor across confirmed data breaches, and phishing specifically remains one of the most common ways attackers get that first foothold.

  • 01 One compromised login can expose shared drives, email history, and internal systems.
  • 02 Attacks scale cheaply. A single campaign can target hundreds of employees at once.
  • 03 Recovery costs go beyond IT time: incident response, legal exposure, and client trust.
Why it's urgent

The weak point isn't technology.

You can patch software. You cannot patch a moment of distraction. That's what makes social engineering different from every other risk on this list, and why the fix has to work with people, not just around them.

Case Study

Popular Phishing Cases

Around the years 2013-2016 marked an era of massive phishing campaigns worldwide. These campaigns targeted at large companies, such as Sony, Google, Facebook, etc., the most prominent of which were:

Sony Entertainment (2014) +
  • Target:Sony Entertainment employees.
  • Phishing Type: Spear Phishing.
  • Overview: Sony fell victim to a spear phishing campaign believed to be lead by the Nation State of North Korea (even though this has not been proven to be true), in which the attackers targeted the companies employees in order to gain access to Sony's confidential data.
  • Consequences: It is estimated that Sony lost around 100 million dollars in sensitive data, including employee private information and unreleased films
  • Reference: "10 Phishing Attacks That Shook the Business World" by Right Hand Cybersecurity
Facebook & Google (2013-2015) +
  • Target: Facebook & Google employees
  • Phishing Type: Spear Phishing
  • Overview: An individual by the name Evaldas Rimasauskas was found responsible for a spear phishing campaign against both Google & Facebook. This individual, along with his co-conspirators, was accused of posing as Taiwan-based company called Quanta Computer, which does business with both companies. Rimasauskas crafted convincing emails with fake invoices targetted at employees that regularly managed multi-million dollar transactions.
  • Consequences: During the span of 2 years, both companies lost a total of 100 million dollars through monetary transactions.
  • Reference: How this scammer used phishing emails to steal over $100 million from Google and Facebook, by Tom Huddleston Jr., CNBC
Attacks on the General Public (2025) +
  • Target: You, the user
  • Phishing Type:Spear Phishing, Smishing, Vishing, Business Email Compromise (BEC) ...
  • Overview: The Internet Crime Complaint Center(IC3) reports a total of 1,008,597 cybercrime complaints in 2025. Of these complaints, 191,561 were complaints of phishing/spoofing attempts, costing millions of dollars to the average american.
  • Consequences:A reported total loss of $215,843,126 in 2025.
  • Reference: Yearly FBI Internet Crime Report

The Defense

Two mitigations, compared honestly.

Neither of these fixes phishing on its own. Together, they cover both halves of the problem: stopping the click, and surviving it if it happens anyway.

Mitigation 01

Multi-Factor Authentication

Even if a password is stolen, a second factor blocks the login. This is the safety net for the moment training doesn't catch.

Effectiveness at stopping account takeoverHigh
Implementation costLow to moderate
Time to roll out company-wideDays to weeks
Mitigation 02

Security Awareness Training

Recurring, realistic phishing simulations and short training moments that teach employees to recognize the attempt before they ever click.

Effectiveness at preventing the clickModerate to high
Implementation costLow, ongoing
Time to roll out company-wideWeeks, recurring
Our Recommendation

Fund both. Neither one covers the other's blind spot.

Training reduces how often employees click in the first place. MFA contains the damage on the times they still do. Layered together, one compromised password stops being a company-wide incident and becomes a non-event.
