The Risk

Phishing turns your own employees into an attack surface.

Attackers don't need to break encryption or crack a firewall. They send an email that looks routine, an invoice, a password reset, a message from IT, and wait for one person to click. Industry breach research consistently points to the human element as a leading factor across confirmed data breaches, and phishing specifically remains one of the most common ways attackers get that first foothold.

  • 01 One compromised login can expose shared drives, email history, and internal systems.
  • 02 Attacks scale cheaply. A single campaign can target hundreds of employees at once.
  • 03 Recovery costs go beyond IT time: incident response, legal exposure, and client trust.
Why it's urgent

The weak point isn't technology.

You can patch software. You cannot patch a moment of distraction. That's what makes social engineering different from every other risk on this list, and why the fix has to work with people, not just around them.

The Defense

Two mitigations, compared honestly.

Neither of these fixes phishing on its own. Together, they cover both halves of the problem: stopping the click, and surviving it if it happens anyway.

Mitigation 01

Multi-Factor Authentication

Even if a password is stolen, a second factor blocks the login. This is the safety net for the moment training doesn't catch.

Effectiveness at stopping account takeoverHigh
Implementation costLow to moderate
Time to roll out company-wideDays to weeks
Mitigation 02

Security Awareness Training

Recurring, realistic phishing simulations and short training moments that teach employees to recognize the attempt before they ever click.

Effectiveness at preventing the clickModerate to high
Implementation costLow, ongoing
Time to roll out company-wideWeeks, recurring
Our Recommendation

Fund both. Neither one covers the other's blind spot.

Training reduces how often employees click in the first place. MFA contains the damage on the times they still do. Layered together, one compromised password stops being a company-wide incident and becomes a non-event.
